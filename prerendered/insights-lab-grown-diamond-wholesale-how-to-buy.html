Where to Buy Lab-Grown Diamonds Wholesale

1 September 2026 · Alvora Diamonds · 4 min read

Lab-grown diamonds are sold wholesale through four channels: direct from manufacturers (mostly India, China, US), online marketplaces (Nivoda, VDB, RapNet), local wholesalers, and trade shows (IIJS, JCK, Hong Kong Jewellery & Gem Fair). The right channel depends on whether you need a rare stone quickly, a repeat specification, memo terms, physical inspection, or a low-inventory digital selling model.

Key takeaways

  • Direct manufacturers are generally strongest for calibrated layouts, matched pairs, custom cuts, and repeat production briefs.
  • Nivoda and VDB offer catalogue breadth and digital workflow tools, but the buyer still needs transaction-level QC.
  • Local wholesalers add speed, physical comparison, and relationship-based exceptions, often at a higher spread.
  • Trade shows are useful for supplier discovery and trust formation, not as a substitute for post-show diligence.

1. Direct from manufacturers

A direct purchase is made from a grower, cutter, polisher, integrated manufacturer, or factory-supported sales office. India, China, and the United States are visible production or commercial centres, but the label "manufacturer" should be tested: ask where growth, cutting, polishing, and grading occur, which functions are outsourced, and who owns the goods.

The advantage is technical dialogue. A buyer can discuss measurements, pair tolerance, layout architecture, cut proportions, colour and clarity, treatment disclosure, certification, rough feasibility, and replenishment. A direct relationship is therefore often the best fit for a designer or manufacturer with a CAD reference or a recurring setting requirement. The trade-off is responsibility for supplier diligence, international shipping, customs, payment exposure, insurance, returns, and internal QC.

Alvora is one direct-from-manufacturer option for buyers seeking a specification-led discussion around calibrated layouts, matched pairs, or custom cuts. The relevant question is not whether a supplier claims the largest inventory; it is whether it can document the agreed requirement, certificate, approval, and commercial terms. 2. Online marketplaces

Nivoda positions itself as a B2B marketplace with natural and lab-grown inventory, consolidated logistics and invoicing, quality-control services, returns, and digital selling tools.1 VDB describes a multi-supplier trade platform with search, pair matching, images, videos, reports, messaging, buy, memo, and hold requests.2 RapNet's successor, Rapaport Trade, is currently presented primarily around natural-diamond sourcing and price tools, so its lab-grown utility should be tested rather than assumed.3

The advantage is breadth without owning every stone. A retailer can quote quickly, show virtual inventory, and order after a sale. The disadvantages are platform dependence, variable supplier service, and less direct manufacturing control. A platform promise about returns, credit, or QC should be checked against the actual terms for lab-grown goods, custom orders, and cross-border transactions.

3. Local wholesalers

Local wholesalers and diamond districts are useful when speed or physical inspection matters. A buyer may compare stones under the same lighting, negotiate a hold or memo, collect urgently, and use an established relationship to resolve an exception. New York, Los Angeles, Antwerp, and other trade centres can provide local knowledge and settlement convenience.

The costs are higher overhead, narrower selection, and possible dependence on one salesperson's inventory. A local supplier adds value when it prevents a missed sale, solves an urgent replacement, or lets a buyer compare stones physically. It is less compelling when it merely resells an easily available stone without adding inspection, credit, or service.

4. Trade shows

IIJS, JCK Las Vegas, and Jewellery & Gem WORLD Hong Kong concentrate manufacturers, wholesalers, designers, and service providers in one place. JCK reported more than 30,000 industry professionals and 1,800 exhibiting brands for its 2026 event; JGW reported more than 55,000 unique buyers and over 3,000 exhibitors for its 2025 edition.4 5

A show is good for comparing cuts, discovering suppliers, and testing technical conversation. It is not proof of delivery performance. After the show, request a formal specification, pro forma invoice, report list, sample or memo terms, return policy, shipping responsibility, and a small test order before committing to a programme.

Which channel should you use?

NeedBest starting channelMain risk to manage
Broad online assortmentMarketplaceVariable supplier quality and platform terms
Calibrated or repeat specificationDirect manufacturerDue diligence and international logistics
Urgent physical comparisonLocal wholesalerHigher spread and narrower range
Supplier discoveryTrade showFollow-up execution and sample bias

A hybrid model is usually more resilient: marketplace breadth, direct manufacturing for exact work, local service for urgent exceptions, and trade shows for discovery. Compare total workflow cost, not only per-carat price.
