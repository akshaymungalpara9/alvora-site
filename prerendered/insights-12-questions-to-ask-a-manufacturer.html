12 Questions to Ask a Lab-Grown Diamond Manufacturer Before You Order

1 September 2026 · Alvora Diamonds · 4 min read

Before ordering laboratory-grown diamonds, ask the manufacturer about specification control, growth method, treatment, certification, commercial terms, logistics, and repeat-order capability. A supplier's catalogue, factory photograph, or low quoted price cannot answer these questions on its own.

Key takeaways

  • Verify the contracting seller, production location, method, treatment, and laboratory paperwork.
  • Ask for the actual QC rule, dimensional tolerances, MOQ, sample or memo terms, and lead-time stages.
  • Separate stone price from certification, freight, insurance, duties, taxes, and customs responsibility.
  • Retain the accepted specification and approval record if repeat supply matters.

The 12-question checklist

1. Who is the contracting seller?

Ask for the legal entity, registered address, invoice details, bank-account name, and the role of any sales office or intermediary. Confirm who owns the goods and who is responsible for a discrepancy or return.

2. Where are growth, cutting, polishing, and grading performed?

A company may combine some functions and outsource others. Ask which steps occur in Surat or another location, who controls the production record, and whether the same specification can be followed across suppliers or facilities.

3. What growth method and treatment apply?

CVD and HPHT are different growth routes. CVD material may be HPHT-annealed after growth, and HPHT stones can have method-specific inclusions or fluorescence features.1 Require method and treatment disclosure in the report or product record where relevant.

4. Which laboratory will issue the paperwork?

Ask whether the stones are IGI, GIA, GCAL, or another laboratory's reports, and whether the format suits your downstream channel. IGI's laboratory-grown report uses familiar 4Cs information; GIA's current colourless service uses Premium or Standard assessments; GCAL 8X adds performance-oriented evidence.2 3 4

5. How do you verify report-to-stone identity?

Ask how report numbers, laser inscriptions, photographs, and shipment records are checked. IGI provides online report verification and laser inscription linkage, but the buyer should still confirm the actual stone against the report.5

6. What exactly is your QC acceptance rule?

Ask for the fields checked before shipment: measurements, colour, clarity, cut, polish, symmetry, fluorescence, treatment, transparency, and visual appearance. For groups, ask for the matching or sorting rule rather than accepting "well matched" as a sufficient description.

7. What are the dimensional tolerances?

For a layout, pair, or custom cut, request the permitted range by measurement. Alvora-specific tolerance values should be confirmed before publication. Clarify whether the range applies to every stone, a sample, or an average, and what happens to an outlier.

8. What is the MOQ?

Ask for the minimum by product type: loose stones, matched pairs, calibrated layouts, melee lots, samples, and custom cuts. Confirm the actual range when the quotation is received.

9. Can I see a sample or use memo terms?

Confirm whether a sample is physical or visual, whether memo is available, the inspection period, return conditions, insurance, shipping responsibility, and exclusions for custom goods. Do not treat "sample available" as a complete commercial term.

10. What is the lead time and what does it include?

Separate feasibility, sourcing, cutting, grading, approval, packing, and dispatch. Actual Alvora timing should be confirmed before publication. Ask what can delay the schedule and how the buyer is notified.

11. What are the landed-cost and delivery terms?

Ask for the Incoterm or equivalent responsibility split, courier, insurance, export documents, certificate charges, freight, destination duties, taxes, and customs clearance. A per-carat quote without these fields is not a reliable landed-cost comparison.

12. Can the specification be repeated?

Ask whether the accepted brief, production lot, report list, images, and QC results are retained for replenishment. Repeatability is not a promise of identical future availability; it is a process for reproducing the agreed requirement and documenting any necessary change.

How to use the answers

Put the answers into a first-order checklist and compare suppliers on the same basis. A direct manufacturer may be more useful than a marketplace for custom cuts or calibrated layouts, while a marketplace may provide more breadth for one-off searches. The right choice depends on the design, urgency, order size, and internal QC capability.

For a first transaction, the most useful evidence is a completed record: the agreed specification, report or lot documentation, approval trail, invoice terms, and delivery outcome. Keep that record so the next order can be compared with the first rather than restarted from an unstructured enquiry.

For a structured first enquiry, use the request-a-quote page and include the product specification, certification need, tolerance, quantity, destination, and required date.
