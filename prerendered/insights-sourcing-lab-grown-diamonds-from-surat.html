Lab-Grown Diamond Manufacturers in Surat: A Buyer's Guide to Sourcing Direct

1 September 2026 · Alvora Diamonds editorial team · 4 min read

The short answer

Sourcing laboratory-grown diamonds direct from Surat can improve technical dialogue and specification control, but buyers still need to verify the manufacturer, documentation, quality process, and landed cost.

What this means for a buyer: Buying direct in Surat improves specification control when you verify the maker; treat verification as part of the price.

For a first-import walkthrough aimed at US retailers, read Sourcing Lab-Grown Diamonds from India: A US Retailer's Guide.

Key takeaways

  • Surat is a major CVD production and processing cluster, but public reactor estimates are not a verified global production share.
  • Direct buying is most useful for repeat specifications, calibrated layouts, matched pairs, and custom cuts.
  • CVD or HPHT should be disclosed and judged by the finished stone, treatment, certificate, and design requirement.
  • Independent certification and supplier-level QC answer different questions; buyers need both for design-specific orders.

What "direct from Surat" actually means

Direct sourcing may mean buying from a grower, cutter and polisher, integrated manufacturer, exporter, or a sales office representing production. These are not interchangeable. Ask who owns or controls the goods, where growth and cutting occur, which functions are outsourced, and who is responsible if a stone fails the agreed specification.

Public evidence supports Surat's importance but not a precise global share. GIA reported an estimate of 4,000–6,000 CVD reactors based in Surat in late 2024, while trade reporting cited higher machine counts depending on date and definition.1 2 Those figures should be treated as evidence of cluster scale, not as proof that any individual supplier has a particular capacity.

Start with the design requirement

A factory-direct conversation is most useful when the buyer brings a technical brief. Include shape, target measurements, colour, clarity, cut expectations, treatment disclosure, quantity, certification laboratory, destination, and whether the goods are stock, a repeat programme, a matched pair, a calibrated layout, melee, or a custom cut. A CAD file or reference stone is often more informative than a broad request for "best price."

For calibrated goods, specify the permitted dimensional variation rather than assuming that a nominal size means identical dimensions. For pairs, define how outline, visual tone, polish, symmetry, and measurements will be compared. For custom cuts, ask whether the requested outline is feasible from available rough before discussing a final delivery promise.

CVD and HPHT: ask for the finished-stone result

CVD grows diamond layer by layer in a low-pressure vacuum chamber; HPHT uses extreme pressure and temperature with a carbon source, seed, and metal flux.3 CVD is strongly associated with Surat, low-nitrogen material, and Type IIa positioning, but it can involve brown as-grown colour and post-growth HPHT annealing. HPHT can produce colourless stones and may be cost-competitive, but buyers should review possible flux inclusions, fluorescence patterns, or phosphorescence.3

The method is not a substitute for inspection. Ask for the actual growth method, treatment status, measurements, cut, colour, clarity, polish, symmetry, and certificate. If a marketing or destination-market claim depends on treatment or origin, require it in the report or supplier record rather than relying on verbal assurance.

Certification and internal QC are different layers

IGI is a practical wholesale baseline because its laboratory-grown report identifies origin and records familiar 4Cs information, with report verification and laser inscription available.4 A report evaluates an individual stone. It does not certify that a parcel is a calibrated layout, that two stones are visually matched, or that a melee lot meets a buyer's sorting standard.

GIA may be appropriate when a downstream channel requires its institutional name, but its current colourless-to-near-colourless laboratory-grown service uses Premium or Standard assessments rather than the conventional natural-diamond format.5 GCAL can be relevant when a buyer wants additional performance evidence, imaging, or an 8X cut route.6

Due diligence before payment

Verify the legal company name, physical address, bank details, export documentation, report numbers, return rights, insurance, ownership of goods, and the treatment of rejected or mismatched stones. Ask for a pro forma invoice that separates the stone price from certification, freight, insurance, duties, and other charges. International direct buying is not automatically cheaper if the buyer carries more internal QC, customs, or exception-handling work.

The practical conclusion

Use Surat direct sourcing when the requirement is repeatable, technical, or design-specific-not simply because a supplier claims factory pricing. A marketplace can be useful for breadth and urgent comparison; a direct manufacturer is more useful for calibrated layouts, matched pairs, custom cuts, and repeat specifications. Start with a small, documented test order and expand only after the supplier performs against the agreed brief.
