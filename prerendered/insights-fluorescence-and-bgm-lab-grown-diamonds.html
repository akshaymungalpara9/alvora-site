Fluorescence and BGM in Lab-Grown Diamonds: What the Terms Mean for Buyers

25 September 2026 · Alvora Diamonds · 4 min read

Fluorescence is the visible glow some diamonds emit under ultraviolet light. BGM stands for brown, green or milky undertones that are not captured by the colour grade alone. Both affect how a stone actually looks, which is why careful buyers confirm them in addition to the 4Cs.

Key takeaways

  • Fluorescence None is the cleanest position for white stones; strong fluorescence can make a stone look hazy in daylight.
  • BGM describes undertones (brown, green, milky) that the colour grade alone does not capture.
  • Neither is visible in the 4Cs summary; both belong in the stone's full description and video.
  • Judge the actual stone, not the label: a verified 360 view shows what the certificate cannot.

Fluorescence, plainly

Under UV light, some diamonds fluoresce, usually blue. In most lighting it is invisible, but strong fluorescence can give a white stone a hazy or oily look in sunlight, which contains UV. A grade of None means the stone shows no measurable fluorescence.

Fluorescence appears on the certificate, but its visual effect varies stone by stone. Alvora ships white stones graded None for fluorescence as standard, so the question does not arise after purchase.

BGM: the undertones between the grades

Two stones with the same colour grade can face up differently because of undertones. Brown and green tinges and milkiness (a sleepy, hazy look) are not part of the colour letter; they are separate observations. BGM is trade shorthand for checking them explicitly.

The protection is simple: ask whether the stone has any brown, green or milky character, and look at the stone itself in good light or in a verified 360 view. Alvora's standard make is no fluorescence and no BGM, stated per stone.

What to ask before you buy

Three questions cover it: what is the fluorescence grade, is there any brown, green or milky character, and can I see the actual stone in a verified video. A seller who grades and films every stone answers all three without effort.
