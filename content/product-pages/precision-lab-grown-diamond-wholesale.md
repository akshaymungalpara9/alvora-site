The starting point is a clear brief. Include shape, measurements, colour and clarity range, quantity, cut requirements, setting constraints, treatment disclosure, certification preference, destination, and CAD or layout reference. Separating essential requirements from preferences makes the quote more useful and exposes feasibility questions early.

Precision, not volume, means focusing on the exact job: how stones must fit together, what the setter needs, which attributes must remain consistent, and what evidence is required for approval. CVD and HPHT can both be valid routes; the appropriate choice depends on the finished specification, not a universal claim that one method is always superior.
