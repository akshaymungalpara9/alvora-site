# Alvora Diamonds: Australian B2B Prospecting Report

**Prepared:** 4 September 2026  
**Scope:** Australian companies that could realistically buy certified, calibrated, matched, or made-to-specification laboratory-grown diamonds for repeat jewellery production or wholesale supply.

## Evidence standard and important limitation

This report is deliberately selective. A company is marked **Verified** only where a public first-party page or strong industry source directly supports the relevant claim. **Strong inference** means the commercial fit is logical but purchasing volume, supplier identity, or import behaviour is not public. **Unconfirmed** means the company is included only as a discovery or event-based lead and should not be treated as a qualified buyer until contacted and verified. No personal email addresses, phone numbers, import relationships, or current suppliers have been invented.

The strongest conclusion is not that every Australian jeweller is a prospect. It is that Alvora should concentrate on **diamond wholesalers, lab-grown specialists, B2B manufacturers, custom-production businesses, and multi-location brands with a meaningful repeat-design pipeline**. Australian market evidence indicates that buyers care about value, quality, individual design, responsible sourcing, education, and trustworthy certification, but the market is divided: lab-grown demand is real while some retailers have become more cautious because of falling resale values.[1] [2]

## 1. Executive summary

Alvora should target Australia through four buyer groups. The first is **specialist lab-grown and diamond wholesalers**: Fairfax & Roberts, Diamond Story, Australian Diamond Importers, JC Jewels, World Shiner, Ellendale Diamonds, and similar firms. They already sell or distribute loose diamonds, understand certificates and stone specifications, and can potentially evaluate a second or specialist supplier. The second is **Australian B2B manufacturers and production houses**, led by Palloys and Golden Mile. These businesses are valuable because Alvora’s calibrated, matched-parcel, and made-to-specification proposition maps directly to repeat production. The third is **lab-grown-led multi-city jewellery brands**, including Novita Diamonds, Temple & Grace, GS Diamonds and Moi Moi. These companies show product demand and operational scale, although they may already have established supplier networks. The fourth is **trade-active diamond and jewellery companies identified through AJF/IJF exhibitor directories**, where trade participation creates a practical route to in-person qualification.[3] [4]

The best initial positioning is **not “cheaper lab-grown diamonds.”** Wholesale prices have experienced severe structural deflation, so commodity inventory is difficult to defend. Alvora should sell procurement outcomes: consistent certificate specifications, matched pairs and parcels, calibrated layouts for repeat designs, reliable make and symmetry, disclosed CVD/HPHT and treatment information, and a responsive Surat production partner capable of custom requirements. This is particularly relevant because public Australian market commentary describes rounds as the largest demand segment, followed by ovals and emerald cuts, while also noting strong interest in personalisation and responsible sourcing.[1]

The first city should be **Sydney**, because it combines the country’s strongest concentration of visible lab-grown specialists, wholesalers, central CBD showroom activity, Moi Moi’s Sydney studio, Temple & Grace’s Sydney workshop, Australian Diamond Importers, Fairfax & Roberts, and the most important national trade event location. **Melbourne is second**, because it has a dense diamond-wholesale and manufacturing ecosystem, including Diamond Story, World Shiner, Golden Mile, and multiple trade-active firms. **Adelaide is strategically important for events**, not because it is the largest buyer market: AJF 2026 was held at the Adelaide Convention Centre, while the 2027 AJF is advertised for Cairns on 10–11 April 2027.[3] [5]

## 2. Top 10 Australian prospects: detailed research

### 1. Moi Moi Fine Jewellery — Sydney, NSW — Buyer Fit 91/100 — Hot

**Evidence status: Verified.** Moi Moi is an Australian-owned family business founded by sisters Lauren and Alana. Its own site says the business introduced Australia to moissanite in 2004 and lab-grown diamonds in 2019, provides worldwide distribution, offers custom design, maintains in-house design capability, and crafts rings in its Sydney studio.[6] The sisters are identified as Managing Directors. This is unusually strong evidence of lab-grown category commitment, custom design, and decision-maker accessibility.

**Why Alvora should approach it:** Moi Moi is a direct fit for certified laboratory-grown centre stones, matched side stones, calibrated layouts, and recurring custom-design production. Its public positioning emphasises ethical created jewels, craftsmanship and value rather than pure commodity pricing. The best angle is a **small controlled sample program** for oval, round and emerald layouts used in custom rings, with IGI certificates, growth-method disclosure, treatment disclosure, and a repeat-order price schedule.

**Contact route:** Public contact and appointment route: [moimoi.com.au/about](https://www.moimoi.com.au/about/) and [contact page](https://www.moimoi.com.au/contact-us/). Decision-makers: Lauren and Alana, Managing Directors. Do not assume which sister handles stone procurement; ask for the person responsible for diamond sourcing or production.

### 2. Novita Diamonds — Sydney / Parramatta / Melbourne / Brisbane / Perth / Adelaide — Buyer Fit 90/100 — Hot

**Evidence status: Verified.** Novita describes itself as Australia’s largest lab-grown diamond jewellery specialist, with more than 2,400 lab-grown diamonds available for viewing in Australia and six Australian showrooms. It offers more than 100 lab-grown ring designs, custom design, multiple diamond shapes, and global expansion. Its story page identifies Iris Arnold as Global CEO and Founder and states that Novita sources directly from manufacturers through a global network of gemmologists.[7]

**Why Alvora should approach it:** Novita has visible inventory depth, multi-city operations, and a public direct-manufacturer sourcing model. It is therefore a high-value benchmark account even if it is difficult to displace current suppliers. Alvora should not pitch generic inventory. It should propose **hard-to-source or repeat-production SKUs**: matched pairs, calibrated side stones, elongated ovals, emeralds, pears, and custom layouts with consistent make across replenishment cycles.

**Contact route:** [novitadiamonds.com](https://novitadiamonds.com/) and the public telephone number +61 1800 503 082. Decision-maker: Iris Arnold, Global CEO and Founder. The first conversation should seek the sourcing, gemmology, or production lead beneath the founder.

### 3. Temple & Grace — Sydney workshop; national showrooms — Buyer Fit 88/100 — Hot

**Evidence status: Verified.** Temple & Grace states that its lab-created rings are made directly by its master jewellers at a Sydney workshop. It offers hundreds of styles, customisation, more than 3,000 certified lab-grown diamonds to view, and showrooms in Sydney, Melbourne, Brisbane, Perth and Adelaide. It identifies itself as a local jewellery-maker, wholesaler and diamond wholesaler and says its laboratory-grown diamonds are certified by GIA and IGI.[8]

**Why Alvora should approach it:** This is a strong production-fit account because its own language connects lab-grown stones with local workshop manufacturing and customisable styles. Alvora can offer **production-friendly stone specifications**, not just single stones: calibrated melee, matched side stones, paired ovals and pears, and replenishable centre-stone ranges for the best-selling settings.

**Contact route:** [templeandgrace.com.au/lab-grown-diamond-rings](https://www.templeandgrace.com.au/lab-grown-diamond-rings) and its showroom/appointment route. The relevant role is likely jewellery production, sourcing, or gemmology; a named public buyer was not verified.

### 4. Palloys — Sydney / national B2B — Buyer Fit 87/100 — High-potential

**Evidence status: Verified.** Palloys describes itself as Australia’s oldest jewellery manufacturer, fabricated-metals and wedding-ring supplier, explicitly servicing the B2B market. It is RJC-certified, offers casting from CAD, customisation, wedding bands, fabricated metals, and lab-diamond finished jewellery.[9]

**Why Alvora should approach it:** Palloys may be both a prospect and a potential channel partner. Its scale and B2B customer base make **consistent calibrated and matched supply** commercially relevant, particularly for wedding bands, finished jewellery and repeat CAD designs. However, Palloys already has substantial capabilities and supplier relationships; Alvora should propose a narrowly defined trial around shapes or parcel specifications that are difficult to maintain locally.

**Contact route:** [palloys.com](https://www.palloys.com/) and the public business contact route. Public LinkedIn routes identify Richard Ashman, General Manager; Christopher Botha, Operations Manager; Sophie Kim, Head of Jewellery CAD/CAM; and Steve Gades, Inventory Manager. The best first contact is the **commercial or inventory function**, with CAD/CAM copied only when the proposal concerns calibrated production.

### 5. Fairfax & Roberts — Sydney, NSW — Buyer Fit 85/100 — Hot

**Evidence status: Verified.** Fairfax & Roberts’ wholesale page states that it provides access to more than 500,000 natural and lab-grown diamonds through trusted international cutters and suppliers. It lists certified stones, pricing transparency, online wholesale purchasing, and bespoke jewellery services.[10]

**Why Alvora should approach it:** This company already understands international supplier networks and offers a wholesale marketplace. Alvora is unlikely to win on broad catalogue size; it may win on **custom procurement, matched parcels, reliable replenishment, and made-to-specification stones** for bespoke or repeat collections. The opening should acknowledge that Fairfax & Roberts already has international supply and ask whether its buyers ever need a second source for exact specifications or time-sensitive custom requirements.

**Contact route:** [fairfaxandroberts.com.au/pages/wholesale-diamonds](https://www.fairfaxandroberts.com.au/pages/wholesale-diamonds), public phone +61 2 9232 8510, and contact/consultation form.

### 6. Diamond Story — Melbourne, VIC — Buyer Fit 84/100 — Hot

**Evidence status: Verified.** Diamond Story describes itself as a Melbourne diamond wholesaler with nearly 20 years of experience, access to more than 2,000,000 natural and lab-grown diamonds, offices in Melbourne and Hong Kong, GIA and IGI certification, custom engagement rings, and ready-to-wear jewellery.[11]

**Why Alvora should approach it:** The Hong Kong office and wholesale positioning indicate an international sourcing mindset. Alvora should lead with **IGI-certified lab-grown diamonds in repeatable shape/size/clarity bands**, especially round, oval, emerald and matched pairs. It should also offer a transparent quote format with certificate, growth method, treatment, make, delivery term and replenishment lead time.

**Contact route:** [diamondstory.com.au](https://www.diamondstory.com.au/), phone 03 9642 3649, and public email info@diamondstory.com.au.

### 7. Australian Diamond Importers — Sydney, NSW — Buyer Fit 81/100 — High-potential

**Evidence status: Verified.** Australian Diamond Importers says it is a family-run wholesale loose-diamond business with more than 60 years of experience, supplies national clients, offers GIA-certified diamonds, and lists lab-created diamonds among its products. It has a Sydney CBD showroom and publicly lists phone numbers and info@australiandiamondimporters.com.au.[12]

**Why Alvora should approach it:** This is a traditional wholesale account with direct evidence of lab-created product inclusion. Its established Sydney CBD operation and national supply claim make it useful for a **wholesale test parcel**, particularly in popular round, oval, princess, pear and cushion shapes. The pitch should focus on adding a specialist Surat source rather than challenging its natural-diamond business.

**Contact route:** [australiandiamondimporters.com.au](https://australiandiamondimporters.com.au/), 155 King Street Sydney, +61 2 9231 3507, +61 400 780 780, info@australiandiamondimporters.com.au.

### 8. GS Diamonds — Sydney / Melbourne / Brisbane / Perth — Buyer Fit 79/100 — High-potential

**Evidence status: Verified.** GS Diamonds offers IGI-certified loose lab-grown diamonds, custom jewellery, engagement rings, tennis bracelets, earrings and national delivery. It has showrooms in Sydney, Melbourne, Brisbane and Perth and publicly lists a telephone contact.[13]

**Why Alvora should approach it:** GS Diamonds demonstrates multi-city demand and a broad product range that uses both centre stones and repeated melee/tennis-bracelet layouts. Alvora should propose **matched pairs and calibrated parcels for tennis, eternity and side-stone designs**, where consistency and availability matter more than one-off inventory.

### 9. Ellendale Diamonds Australia — Perth, WA — Buyer Fit 76/100 — High-potential

**Evidence status: Strong inference.** Ellendale has a trade-account registration route, a “become a stockist” pathway, diamond and jewellery collections, and public sales contact details. Its website includes IGI lab filters in its diamond-search infrastructure, but its visible commercial story is also strongly associated with Australian, Argyle and traceable diamonds.[14]

**Why Alvora should approach it:** This is not a confirmed lab-grown volume buyer. It is worth a controlled approach because it already operates a trade/stockist model and may need a lab-grown line or alternative supplier for value-sensitive accounts. The pitch should explicitly respect its traceability positioning and offer **documented IGI-certified stones with clear origin, CVD/HPHT and treatment disclosures**.

**Contact route:** [ellendalediamonds.com.au](https://www.ellendalediamonds.com.au/), sales@ellendalediamonds.com.au, +61 8 6111 1961.

### 10. Golden Mile Jewellery Manufacturers — Rowville, Melbourne, VIC — Buyer Fit 75/100 — High-potential

**Evidence status: Verified manufacturing; lab-grown demand unconfirmed.** Golden Mile is family-owned and operated since 1990, manufactures gold jewellery for the industry, and states that most styles are made in its Melbourne factory. It offers custom colour, length and size requests and publishes sales@goldenmile.com.au and +61 3 9753 3977.[15]

**Why Alvora should approach it:** Golden Mile’s repeat-production and customisation model is a strong fit for calibrated and matched supply, but its visible product emphasis is chains, bangles, earrings, pendants and 9ct/silver-filled jewellery rather than diamond-heavy engagement pieces. It should be approached with a **small pilot around diamond-set earrings, pendants or custom industry orders**, not with a large centre-stone catalogue.

**Contact route:** [goldenmile.com.au](https://www.goldenmile.com.au/), sales@goldenmile.com.au, +61 3 9753 3977. Public LinkedIn search results identify Maison Erdogan as Director/CFO; use that only as a public LinkedIn route and verify the current role before outreach.

## 6. Best jewellery industry clusters

The first cluster is **Sydney CBD and Queen Victoria Building / central-city jewellery activity**. It combines Australian Diamond Importers, Fairfax & Roberts’ flagship and consultation model, Moi Moi’s flagship store and Sydney studio, Novita’s Sydney and Parramatta operations, Temple & Grace’s Sydney workshop, and GS Diamonds’ Sydney showroom. The objective should be a concentrated two-day appointment program rather than random store visits.

The second cluster is **Melbourne CBD and the broader south-eastern manufacturing belt**. Diamond Story, World Shiner and JC Jewels provide wholesale and market-intelligence access, while Golden Mile in Rowville provides a manufacturing route. Melbourne is particularly valuable for conversations about production specifications, custom work, and the difference between natural and laboratory-grown demand.

The third cluster is **trade-event ecosystems**. AJF’s official pages describe a trade-only event connecting retailers, designers, wholesalers and suppliers. Its 2025/2026 directories include Palloys, Golden Mile, Ellendale, J.S. Landau, RR Diamonds, DJ Diamond Designs, Prana Diamonds, Blumo​on Diamond and other relevant names.[3] IJF’s 2025 directory includes The Diamond Port, Ethereal Green Diamond, Gen Z Diamonds, J.S. Landau, Diamond Sensation, Classic Grown Diamonds, Prana, Palloys, Ellendale and Ausgem Labs.[4]

## 8. Competitor and supplier insights

Australian buyers appear to value **certification, visible quality, price transparency, customisation, ethical sourcing, local service, and education**. Fairfax & Roberts publicly lists wholesale prices and certificates; Diamond Story emphasises GIA/IGI certification and international sourcing; Temple & Grace emphasises local making, certified lab-grown diamonds and customisation; Novita emphasises quality selection by gemmologists, local stock and custom manufacturing; Moi Moi emphasises created jewels, ethical values and craftsmanship.[6] [7] [8] [10] [11]

The market also shows a tension Alvora must address. Public trade commentary reports that almost 40% of Australian retailers surveyed considered lab-grown jewellery important, while 67% of suppliers disagreed with that statement. The same source reports concerns about falling lab-grown value, while also describing rounds as the leading shape and demand for ovals, emeralds, personalisation and responsible sourcing.[1] The commercial implication is that buyers may like the category but dislike **inventory risk**. Alvora should therefore offer small, repeatable, specification-led buys rather than pressure buyers to carry large undifferentiated stock.

Public evidence of named incumbent suppliers is limited. Moi Moi was reported to source stones from a trader, but that historical report also said origin was not always known; this is not a basis for claiming a current supplier relationship.[1] Novita says it sources directly from manufacturers through a global gemmologist network, but does not name those manufacturers.[7] Fairfax & Roberts refers to trusted international cutters and suppliers without naming them.[10] The correct competitive strategy is therefore **not to allege supplier weakness**. It is to ask where the buyer experiences gaps: exact make, matched pairs, replenishment, fancy-shape availability, treatment disclosure, or delivery speed.

## 10. Outreach strategy by buyer segment

### Lab-grown specialists
Lead with category depth and replenishment. A useful offer is a 10–20 stone sample set or digital inventory shortlist covering rounds, ovals, emeralds and pears, followed by a test order. The message should say that Alvora is not asking the buyer to replace its current range; it is offering a second source for **repeatable specifications and matched production parcels**.

### Diamond wholesalers
Lead with procurement efficiency. Provide a one-page quote format containing certificate number, laboratory, growth method, treatment, measurements, fluorescence, polish, symmetry, video availability, delivery term and return/claim process. Ask which specifications are difficult to source consistently and offer to quote those first.

### B2B manufacturers and workshops
Lead with production reliability. Ask for a CAD or stone layout and return a matched/calibrated proposal. Discuss tolerance, matching, make consistency, parcel sizes, replenishment and lead times. This segment is the clearest fit for Alvora’s made-to-specification positioning.

### National chains
Do not start with a founder or store manager. Use corporate procurement, merchandise, category or supply-chain routes. Expect vendor onboarding, compliance, insurance, documentation and pricing hurdles. Treat Michael Hill and Shiels as strategic long-cycle accounts, not immediate wins.

### Emerging designers
Use a low-friction sample and content approach. Offer a small custom run and a transparent minimum order rather than a full wholesale catalogue. Keep them in Tier 3 unless there is evidence of collection launches, multiple stockists or repeat production.
