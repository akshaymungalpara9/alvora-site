Vetting the manufacturer is one job; checking the order is another. This checklist covers the second — the twelve questions to have answered in writing before money or goods move. If you have already read [our companion piece on vetting a manufacturer](/insights/12-questions-to-ask-a-manufacturer), treat this as the order-level sequel: everything here assumes you have chosen the supplier and now need the transaction to go cleanly.

## Key takeaways

- Every answer you accept verbally will be renegotiated from memory if something goes wrong — get it in writing.
- Payment terms, price validity and the dispute window matter more on order one than on order fifty.
- Approval images and certificate verification are part of the order, not courtesies around it.
- None of these questions should be uncomfortable to ask; discomfort with any of them is itself an answer.

## Payment and price

**1. What are the payment terms for a first order, and when do they change?** New relationships typically run on advance payment; ask what volume or history unlocks credit terms, memo or split payments, and get the escalation path stated.

**2. How long is my quotation valid?** Lab-grown pricing can move quickly. A quote without a validity date is a quote you cannot plan against — ask for a stated window and a written confirmation mechanism for any change before you commit.

**3. What exactly does the price include — certificates, packaging, insurance to which point?** Compare quotes on a like-for-like landed basis, not on the line-item price alone.

## Specification and approval

**4. Can I approve a sample or images before full production or dispatch?** For stock goods, ask for images of the actual stones; for bespoke or matched work, agree the approval-images step as a formal gate before dispatch.

**5. Which laboratory certifies the goods, and can I verify the certificates independently?** Confirm whether stones are IGI- or GIA-certified, and verify report numbers on the laboratory's own portal before paying — this takes minutes and prevents most fraud exposure.

**6. What tolerances apply to size, colour and clarity on parcel goods?** "Calibrated" and "matched" need stated numbers behind them. If the answer is a shrug, the specification is a shrug.

## Timing, shipping and insurance

**7. What is the realistic dispatch time for this order — and is it stock or bespoke?** Stock and standard orders commonly dispatch in 2–3 working days; bespoke custom-cut or calibrated-layout production runs take longer, typically around 7–10 working days. A supplier quoting stock speed on bespoke work is a supplier quoting hope.

**8. How is the shipment insured, from where to where, and can I see the policy details?** High-value parcels should carry door-to-door cover you can verify, with the claim procedure agreed before dispatch.

**9. Who is the importer of record, and which Incoterms apply?** Confirm whether goods arrive EXW, CIF or DDP, and align the answer with your customs broker's expectations before the airway bill exists.

## After delivery

**10. What is the discrepancy-reporting window?** Agree in writing how many days you have to inspect and report weight, grade, quantity or damage issues — and what evidence is accepted.

**11. What is the remedy for a confirmed discrepancy — replacement, credit or refund?** The strongest suppliers answer this before being asked; the answer reveals how they expect to be held.

**12. What are the conditions for returns or balance stock?** Ask whether unused certified stones or parcel balances can be returned or exchanged, on what terms, and within what period. Even a "no" stated clearly is better than an assumption discovered later.

## Why these questions, and not the usual ones

Most pre-order advice concentrates on the seller: their history, their factory, their reputation. Those questions matter, and we cover them in [our companion article on vetting a manufacturer](/insights/12-questions-to-ask-a-manufacturer) — but they are questions you ask once. The twelve questions here are different in kind: they concern the transaction you are about to enter, and they are worth re-asking, in some form, on every significant order. Credentials answer "can this supplier be trusted?"; these questions answer "will this specific order, on these specific terms, behave the way I assume it will?" Both matter. The second is the one buyers forget.

## A one-paragraph version for your first enquiry

If twelve questions feels like a lot for an opening email, compress it: *"Please confirm in writing: payment terms and quotation validity; certification laboratory with verifiable report numbers; stated tolerances for size, colour and clarity; dispatch time for this exact order (stock or bespoke); insurance coverage and Incoterms; and your discrepancy, remedy and returns policy."* A manufacturer accustomed to B2B trade will answer all of it in a single reply — and that reply is itself useful vetting data.

## In closing

First orders in any new supply relationship carry an outsized share of the risk, and almost all of that risk lives in the unasked question rather than the unreliable supplier. These twelve questions take one email to send and one careful reply to settle; keep both on file, because they become the reference point for every order that follows. Good manufacturers welcome them — a precise buyer is cheaper to serve than a vague one.
