Matched pairs, melee, and calibrated layouts are different wholesale formats: pairs coordinate two stones, melee supplies small stones by lot, and layouts coordinate a larger design-specific group. Choosing the right format starts with the jewellery design, not with the supplier's product menu.

## Key takeaways

- A matched pair coordinates two stones; melee is a sorted lot; a layout coordinates a larger design-specific group.
- Pairs need joint visual and dimensional review, while melee needs lot-level sorting and packaging rules.
- Calibrated layouts should connect actual measurements and reports to the design or CAD requirement.
- The correct format depends on the setting, quantity, acceptance rule, and repeat-order need.

## The short distinction

A matched pair is usually two stones intended to work together, such as earrings or side-by-side accents. Melee is a lot of small stones sorted into a defined size and quality range for pavé, halos, clusters, or repeated settings. A calibrated layout is a coordinated group whose measurements and visual relationships are intended to fit a particular design or production architecture.

## Comparison table

| Format | Typical design use | Main QC question | Documentation focus | Ordering brief |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Matched pair | Earrings, paired accents, symmetrical settings | Do the two stones work together visually and dimensionally? | Individual reports where applicable, pair measurements, joint images | Shape, pair tolerance, colour, clarity, setting, approval |
| Melee | Pavé, halos, clusters, repeated small-stone work | Does the lot stay within the requested size and quality band? | Lot reference, sorting record, weight, packaging, rejects | Size band, colour, clarity, sorting, pack, price basis |
| Calibrated layout | Repeated design, bracelet, collection, complex setting | Does the group fit the design and remain coherent? | Individual reports where applicable plus layout sheet and approval | Nominal dimensions, group tolerance, quantity, CAD, substitutions |

## Matched pairs: compare the stones together

Equal carat weight is not enough. Length, width, depth, outline, faceting, fluorescence, polish, symmetry, and face-up appearance can differ between stones with similar weights. A pair request should define the acceptable difference and explain which features matter to the mounting. IGI reports can identify the individual laboratory-grown stones, but a report does not certify the pair as a visual unit.[1](https://www.igi.org/reports/lab-grown-diamond-report/)

Ask for report numbers linked to each stone, a measurement comparison, and an image or video showing the pair together. For rounds, ovals, and cushions, the matching rule may need to address outline and proportions differently. A pair selected for earrings may need closer visual balance than a pair used as independent side stones.

## Melee: buy the lot, not the promise

Melee is evaluated at lot level. Define the size band by sieve, diameter, carat range, or the supplier's agreed method. Also state colour, clarity, cut or finish expectations, treatment disclosure, minimum lot weight, and the acceptable proportion of off-size or rejected stones. Packaging should identify the lot, size band, weight, quality description, and any QC reference.

Per-carat pricing needs a clear basis. Is the price for shipped goods, accepted goods, or an estimate before final sorting? Are shortages, rejects, substitutions, certification, packing, freight, and insurance included? A low nominal price can be difficult to compare if suppliers use different yield and acceptance conventions.

## Calibrated layouts: connect stones to the design

A layout begins with nominal measurements, shape, quality, quantity, and setting architecture. It may include a CAD file or setter drawing. The buyer should state the permitted ±mm range for the relevant dimensions; the exact supplier range should remain `TODO(alvora)` until confirmed. An independent laboratory report can document individual stones, but group-level QC requires a layout sheet, actual measurements, and buyer approval.

For collection work, the most valuable record is often the accepted specification. It allows a future request to refer back to the same dimensions, quality logic, matching rule, and documentation standard. That does not guarantee identical future supply; rough availability and market conditions can change. It does make the request more precise.

## Which format should you choose?

Choose a **matched pair** when the design needs two coordinated stones. Choose **melee** when the setter needs many small stones and can work from a lot-level sorting standard. Choose a **calibrated layout** when multiple stones must fit a defined design or repeat programme. Some projects need more than one format: a halo may combine a centre stone, a calibrated layout, and melee accents.
