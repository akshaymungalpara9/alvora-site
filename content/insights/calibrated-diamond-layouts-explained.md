A calibrated diamond layout is a coordinated group of stones selected or produced to fit a design within agreed dimensional, visual, and documentation criteria. The term is often used loosely, so buyers should ask what is actually calibrated, how the tolerance is measured, and whether the supplier is describing a stock parcel or a design-specific layout.

## Key takeaways

- "Calibrated" should mean an agreed dimensional and visual acceptance rule, not merely a nominal size label.
- Individual laboratory reports and group-level supplier QC answer different questions.
- Buyers should define tolerances, substitutions, approval evidence, and repeat-order records before ordering.
- IGI paperwork can support stone identity and comparison, but it does not certify a layout as a group.

## Calibrated does not mean identical

A layout can contain stones with the same nominal size while their length, width, depth, outline, table, faceting, polish, symmetry, or face-up appearance differs. Jewellery settings may expose those differences, particularly in repeated pavé, halos, bracelets, earrings, and architectural designs. The relevant question is not whether every value is mathematically identical; it is whether the permitted variation is suitable for the design and consistently checked.

## What should be in the specification

The brief should include the shape, nominal length and width, acceptable depth, colour and clarity range, quantity, cut expectations, treatment disclosure, certification preference, and setting application. If a CAD file or setter drawing exists, attach it. Identify which requirements are fixed and which are negotiable. For example, a maximum width may be essential to a channel while a broader colour range may be acceptable.

The buyer should also define the acceptance rule. Will the stones be accepted by caliper measurement, report data, image review, physical inspection, or a combination? Will visual matching be assessed under specified lighting? Will a substitution require approval? These questions belong in the order record, not only in a WhatsApp conversation.

## How grading works

There are two separate levels of review. An independent laboratory report evaluates an individual diamond. IGI's laboratory-grown report can identify origin and record shape, measurements, carat weight, colour, clarity, polish, symmetry, and cut information where applicable.[1](https://www.igi.org/reports/lab-grown-diamond-report/) Report verification and laser inscription can link a report number to the stone.[2](https://www.igi.org/Verify-Your-Report/)

The layout itself needs supplier QC. A report does not certify that a set of stones fits a mounting or that the group is visually coherent. A sensible layout record connects each stone to its report number, measurements, production or lot reference, approval image, and buyer decision. The group can then be evaluated against the design requirement rather than treated as a series of unrelated certificates.

## Common tolerance questions

| Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Is the tolerance for length, width, depth, or all three? | Different settings may be sensitive to different dimensions. |
| Is it a maximum range or an average? | An average can hide outliers that affect assembly. |
| Is the range measured before or after final grading? | Measurement records and reports may be produced at different stages. |
| Does it apply to every stone or a sample? | A sample standard is not the same as lot-wide control. |
| What happens when a stone falls outside it? | Rejection, substitution, rework, or buyer approval should be defined. |

## IGI paperwork and layout approval

IGI is useful because a buyer can compare individual stones in familiar 4Cs language. For layouts, however, the paperwork should be supplemented by a group sheet showing the requested dimensions, actual measurements, matching criteria, report numbers, and approval status. If the destination market or retailer requires GIA or GCAL, specify that before selection; the laboratory format and cost may affect the workflow.[3](https://www.gia.edu/gem-lab-service/laboratory-grown-diamond) [4](https://www.gcalusa.com/gcal-8x-cut-grade.html)

## How to order without ambiguity

Send the design application first, then the stone requirements. Ask the supplier to confirm feasibility, MOQ, tolerance, lead time, certification route, sample or memo availability, and the treatment and growth-method fields that will be disclosed. Request an approval image or video showing the group together. For a repeat programme, keep the accepted layout sheet as the reference for future replenishment.
