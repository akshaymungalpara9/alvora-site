Alvora supplies lab-grown diamonds from Surat with IGI and GIA certification available according to the buyer's specification. For a jewellery business, certification is not a decorative badge: it is a traceable record that should correspond to the individual stone, the agreed report route, and the technical details used for purchasing and production.

## What the certification workflow covers

The workflow begins with the order brief. The buyer identifies whether IGI or GIA is required, together with the shape, size, quantity, colour, clarity, cut or finish, destination market, and any layout, pair, or custom-cut requirement. Stating the certification body at the start prevents a report expectation from being treated as an afterthought. Alvora's confirmed certification bodies are IGI and GIA; the applicable route is confirmed for the specific order.

The second step is report-to-stone identity. A report belongs to an individual diamond, so the report number, stone record, and finished-stone measurements should remain associated throughout the order. Where a parcel contains multiple stones, each stone should be reconciled with its own documentation or with the agreed parcel-level record. A certificate can support identity and grading information, but it does not by itself prove that a group is a calibrated layout or a visually matched pair.

The third step is technical review. IGI laboratory-grown diamond reports can record laboratory-grown origin, measurements, carat weight, colour, clarity, polish, symmetry, and cut information where applicable.[1] GIA is also a confirmed certification body for Alvora's trade enquiries. Buyers should state the report format required by their retail, manufacturing, or destination-market channel rather than assume that every report route covers the same fields in the same way.

The fourth step is buyer verification. On receipt, compare the report number with the documentation supplied for the order and, where applicable, verify the report through the relevant laboratory's own report-verification process. Confirm that the stone's identity and recorded measurements correspond to the report. For a layout or pair, review the stones together as a group as well as checking each individual report. The group requirement remains a production and QC question, not something a single laboratory report can answer.

## What certification does and does not establish

Certification provides independent documentation for the individual stone. It can help a buyer compare 4Cs information, report the laboratory-grown origin, and maintain a clearer purchasing record. It does not replace a production brief, a dimensional acceptance rule, a visual review, or written agreement about a matched parcel. Those controls matter when the stone must fit a setting, repeat a design, or work beside another stone.

Certification also does not turn an unspecified commercial promise into a guarantee. The buyer should confirm the required laboratory, report fields, treatment or growth-method disclosure where relevant, quantity, inspection method, approval point, and any correction or rework provision in the quotation. The Master Input Sheet does not confirm a universal rework guarantee, so no blanket promise should be inferred from the presence of an IGI or GIA report.

## A practical buyer checklist

| Check | What to confirm |
|---|---|
| Laboratory | Whether the order requires IGI or GIA certification. |
| Identity | Report number and its association with the individual stone. |
| Technical record | Measurements, carat, colour, clarity, polish, symmetry, and cut information where applicable. |
| Group requirement | Whether a layout, pair, or parcel also needs dimensional and visual acceptance. |
| Order record | Shape, quantity, destination, timing, and any CAD or reference material. |
| Final review | Report-to-stone correspondence before the goods enter the buyer's inventory. |
